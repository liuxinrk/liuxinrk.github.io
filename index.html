Research interest

Plankton is a term used to describe the diverse group of microscopic organisms that drift in water bodies, including oceans, lakes, and rivers. These organisms play a vital role in the functioning of aquatic ecosystems, serving as the primary source of food for many species of fish and other higher trophic level organisms. The abundance and distribution of plankton are influenced by a complex interplay of physical, chemical, and biological processes, and understanding these dynamics is crucial for predicting and managing the health of aquatic ecosystems.

My research in plankton ecology is ongoing in several key areas, including the effects of environmental factors such as light, temperature, and water currents on plankton populations, the role of biological processes such as predation and competition in shaping the structure of plankton communities, and the impact of human activities such as pollution and climate change on plankton populations. Additionally, new methods and technologies are being developed to better understand plankton populations and the dynamics of their interactions with their environment. My ongoing research in plankton ecology presents opportunities for new discoveries and advances that have the potential to improve our understanding of aquatic ecosystems and the role of plankton in these systems.
